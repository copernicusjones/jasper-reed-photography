How to Deliver a Photo Gallery Your Clients Will Actually Love

Photography Business Resources · Free Guide

The shoot is done, the edits are finished — now the part that decides whether you get rebooked and referred: delivery. Here's the system that makes the experience feel premium and keeps clients coming back.

1. Cull First, Edit Second

Clients don't want 500 photos. They want 30–50 strong selects. Cull ruthlessly: blurry, duplicate expressions, bad lighting cuts. A tighter gallery feels intentional and curated, not overwhelming.

2. Use a Professional Delivery Platform

Dropbox and Google Drive are fine for file transfer, but clients don't get a gallery experience. Use a dedicated delivery tool (Pixieset, ShootProof, or a simple web gallery) — it gives you brand control, download tracking, and a professional first impression.

3. Set Expectations Before Delivery

Tell clients when to expect the gallery, how many images to expect, and how long the download link stays active. "You'll get 30–40 edited selects in 7–10 business days, with a 30-day download window." Surprise-free delivery is happy delivery.

4. Include a Personal Note

A two-sentence email with the gallery link, the image count, and a genuine line about the shoot ("We had great light that day") goes further than you'd think. It turns a transaction into a relationship.

5. Follow Up After Download

Three days after delivery: "Did everything come through OK? Any adjustments?" Then, after the client has had time to use the images: "Would you be open to leaving a quick testimonial?" Timing matters — ask after the win, not at the moment of delivery.
